OPTIMIZATION · GEOMETRY

Minkowski optimization

Layout optimization using Minkowski differences, signed distance functions, and geometric constraints to handle shape placement and non-overlap in architectural design.

Built with JAX · Optax · NumPy · SciPy · Plotly · Streamlit · rhino3dm

Summary

This work applies Minkowski penalties, a method introduced by Jiří Minarčík, Sam Estep, Wode Ni and Keenan Crane (SIGGRAPH 2024 ↗). Two shapes A and B overlap exactly when the origin lies inside their Minkowski difference A ⊕ (−B). Its signed distance function, evaluated at the origin, therefore measures how far apart the shapes are, or how deeply they overlap. A penalty built from this distance is zero when a constraint holds and has a nonzero gradient whenever it is violated. It also works with automatic differentiation. The same construction expresses non-overlap, containment within a boundary, and exact padding. The constraints are enforced with an exterior-point method: the layout may start infeasible, and the penalty weight grows until every constraint holds.

MACHINE LEARNING · GEOMETRY

Differentiable rasterization

A differentiable vector-graphics rasterizer in JAX, used to fit images with simple shapes by gradient descent and to generate shape layouts from floorplan masks. Builds on Sasha Rush’s Differentiable Rasterization ↗.

Built with JAX · Optax · Chalk · pycairo · Streamlit

Summary

The starting point is the rasterizer from Sasha Rush’s Differentiable Rasterization ↗ notebook, built on the Chalk ↗ diagrams library. I debugged it to run on a current JAX and NumPy stack. Gradients reach shape boundaries through the Leibniz integral rule, so the position, size, rotation and colour of each shape can be optimised directly. Two experiments build on it. The first approximates the coloured regions of an image with circles and rectangles. The second is an amortised, conditional model: an encoder and learned slot embeddings map a floorplan mask to a fixed set of shapes in a single forward pass, trained across a dataset.

OPTIMIZATION · ECONOMICS

Masterplan and spatial modelling

Multi-objective optimization for masterplans, spatial equilibrium economic modelling, and geometric approaches to design constraints. The spatial model builds on Ahlfeldt, Redding, Sturm and Wolf, “The Economics of Density: Evidence from the Berlin Wall” (Econometrica 2015 ↗).

Built with NumPy · SciPy · pandas · Plotly · Streamlit

Summary

A quantitative spatial equilibrium model of Richmond upon Thames, built on the framework of Ahlfeldt, Redding, Sturm and Wolf (Econometrica 2015 ↗). Working residents choose where to live and where to work, either in the borough or in Central London, reached by rail. Floor prices, local wages, residents and jobs are then solved jointly in general equilibrium. The model is calibrated to house prices by postcode district, ONS neighbourhood incomes and VOA floor space. It is then used to test counterfactuals such as new river crossings.

MACHINE LEARNING · STATISTICS

Probabilistic and causal models

Bayesian hierarchical modelling, hidden Markov models, Gaussian processes, and gradient boosting with SHAP explanations. Causal inference with causal diagrams, identification of causal effects, and do-calculus.

Study (PDF) ↗

Built with PyMC3 · SHAP

Summary

From my study Information reconstruction with Bayesian machine learning (LSE, 2025). The task is to recover an unobserved black-and-white image from J independent copies, each corrupted by Gaussian noise. The image is modelled as an Ising Markov random field, with each pixel linked to its neighbours and to the matching pixel in every noisy copy. The posterior is approximated by mean-field variational inference, maximising the ELBO. Each extra copy adds evidence to every pixel at a cost linear in J. Tested on M. C. Escher’s woodcut Sky and Water I (1938; © The M. C. Escher Company B.V.), reconstructions keep improving from 1 to 10 to 100 copies. The same study also uses a Gaussian process with a Matérn and white-noise kernel to reconstruct missing readings from building temperature sensors.

NeurIPS · 2026Conference

Virtual Double Oracle: Faster Convergence by Leveraging Strategies That Were Watching All Along

Ruilan Wang, Francisco Aristi Reina, Katerina Papadaki

arXiv ↗Code ↗

Built with OpenSpiel (PSRO) · NumPy · SciPy · CVXOPT · Matplotlib

Summary

Double Oracle methods approximate Nash equilibria by growing each player’s strategy population with best responses and running multiplicative-weights learning over it. Every time a strategy is discovered it is normally reset to a uniform prior. Virtual Double Oracle instead gives it the weight it would have earned had it been in play from the start — recoverable exactly from its payoff against the time-averaged opponent. This removes the ln k factor from Online Double Oracle’s rate, giving O(√(k/T)) exploitability, and a discounted variant (dVDO) lowers exploitability across matrix games, poker and Goofspiel.

ALA · 2026Conference

Machine Learning in a Social Deduction Game

Francisco Aristi Reina, Steffen Issleib, Bernhard von Stengel

Paper (PDF) ↗

Built with PyTorch (ToT-ESCHER) · Llama 4 Maverick (LLM agents) · Flask · FastAPI

Summary

AI agents play Temple of Terror, a hidden-role team game in which players declare their cards — possibly lying — and choose whose card is uncovered next. The paper compares ToT-ESCHER, a neural counterfactual-regret learner adapted to two teams, with prompt-engineered LLM agents of varying reasoning depth, measuring win rates against random play, common human heuristics and PPO-trained best responses.

AAG · 2025Conference

From Constraints to Configurations: An Integer Linear Programming Approach for Architectural Space-Planning

Francisco Aristi Reina, Marios Tsiliakos, Marcin Kosicki, Chau Nguyen, Martha Tsigkari

Paper (PDF) ↗

Built with CVXPY · SciPy

Summary

Space planning is recast as an integer linear program: given a building’s floorplates, the model packs residential units to meet gross leasable area, unit-mix targets, spatial distribution and typology rules, returning globally optimal or near-optimal configurations. On a high-rise residential case study the method keeps area-utilisation error below 2% while matching the target unit mix exactly, and plugs into standard CAD workflows for rapid iteration.

LSE · 2022MSc thesis

An Explanation of Computability and Structural Properties of the Turing Degrees

Francisco Aristi Reina · MSc dissertation, Department of Mathematics, London School of Economics

Thesis (PDF) ↗
Summary

The dissertation starts from Turing’s 1936 model of computation. It uses Gödel numbering to build a Universal Turing Machine and to exhibit incomputable sets such as the halting problem. Adding oracles gives Turing’s relative computability, and grouping sets that compute each other gives the Turing degrees, Post’s partial order of degrees of unsolvability. Following Kleene and Post (1954), the thesis proves that the order is not linear (there are incomparable degrees) and that there is an infinite independent sequence of degrees. It follows that every finite partial order embeds into the degrees, so the existential (∃₁) theory of the structure is decidable. Finally, the thesis shows that the degrees form an upper semi-lattice, where any two degrees have a least upper bound given by the join A ⊕ B, but not a lattice.
